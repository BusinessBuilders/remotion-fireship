# Five Simple Hacks to Build Websites With AI — Voiceover Script

## Chapter 1: The Problem

### Section 1: Why AI Websites All Look The Same (0:04 - 0:11)
"Most AI-built websites fail the same way. One vague prompt goes in, and out comes the same floating gradient, the same soft-copy hero, and the same fake premium vibe. It looks polished for about three seconds, and then your brain realizes it could be for literally any company."

### Section 2: The Real Advantage (0:11 - 0:17)
"That is the irony. AI absolutely CAN make you faster. GitHub has cited research showing task speedups up to fifty-five percent, and a November 2024 study found Copilot users had a fifty-three point two percent higher likelihood of passing all test cases. Speed is real. Generic output is optional."

### Section 3: The Workflow That Fixes It (0:17 - 0:24)
"The fix is not mystical design talent. It is workflow. Good AI websites usually come from the same pipeline: a clear brief, real brand assets, design rules, the AI builder itself, and then one last human review pass where the bad stuff gets cut."

## Chapter 2: Better Inputs

### Section 4: Hack 1: Write The Brief Once (0:24 - 0:31)
"Hack number one: write the brief once. Do NOT start with build me a website. Start with who the site is for, what the brand should feel like, what stack to use, what to avoid, and what done is supposed to look like."

### Section 5: Give It A Brain (0:31 - 0:39)
"Think of that file like a brain transplant for the project. In Claude Code, that might be CLAUDE dot M D. In another builder, it might just be your saved project prompt. Either way, the point is the same: stop making the model rediscover your taste every single run."

### Section 6: Hack 2: Add Taste On Purpose (0:39 - 0:46)
"Hack number two: add taste on purpose. AI is great at remixing patterns. It is much worse at inventing a strong visual direction from nothing. So give it a design system, a front-end skill, a gallery of references, or a list of anti-patterns before it touches the UI."

### Section 7: Spend Less Time Guessing (0:46 - 0:51)
"Open source developer Claudio Wunder put it well: he spends less time on trial and error, and more time making sure the result is secure and performant. That is the shift. Less guessing. More directing."

## Chapter 3: Better Context

### Section 8: Hack 3: Feed Assets, Not Adjectives (0:51 - 0:58)
"Hack number three: feed assets, not adjectives. Modern and professional means almost nothing to a model. A logo, color palette, type choices, screenshots, copy doc, and brand guide? That is actual context. That is how the page stops looking like everybody else's."

### Section 9: Build A Context Stack (0:58 - 1:05)
"The highest-leverage move is building a context stack. Logo. Brand guide. Reference pages. Copy notes. Maybe even screenshots of layouts you like. Layer enough specifics together, and the model finally has something stronger than the default internet aesthetic."

### Section 10: Hack 4: Build In Passes (1:05 - 1:13)
"Hack number four: build in passes. Ask for structure first. Then style. Then copy polish. Then responsive cleanup. Then QA. When you demand the final website in one shot, you are basically telling the AI to average every decision together into one bland first draft."

### Section 11: Prompt Like A Director (1:13 - 1:22)
"The better pattern is directing. First, build the section structure only. Next, restyle it using the brand guide. Then rewrite the hero for the real audience. Then fix mobile. Then list every remaining problem. Same model. Same project. Way better output because the thinking is staged."

## Chapter 4: Better Output

### Section 12: One-Shot Prompt vs Branded Workflow (1:22 - 1:30)
"This is the split. One-shot prompting gives you a disposable mockup. A branded workflow gives you something that actually feels shippable. The difference is not just prettier colors. It is stronger hierarchy, tighter copy, cleaner spacing, and fewer weird AI decisions."

### Section 13: Hack 5: Review Like A Creative Director (1:30 - 1:37)
"Hack number five: review like a creative director. Your value is not typing every div by hand anymore. Your value is judgment. Cut the filler. Fix the rhythm. Push the headline harder. Make the model earn its place on the page."

### Section 14: The Four-Pass Refinement Loop (1:37 - 1:43)
"A simple loop works well here. Pass one: structure. Pass two: visual direction. Pass three: copy and hierarchy. Pass four: mobile and polish. By the time you hit that fourth pass, the website usually stops feeling AI-generated and starts feeling intentional."

### Section 15: This Works Beyond One Tool (1:43 - 1:50)
"And yes, this works beyond one tool. Claude Code, v0, Lovable, Bolt, whatever. In 2026 the teams getting the best AI websites are not the ones with the fanciest prompt. They are the ones giving the model better context and doing stricter review."

### Section 16: The Takeaway (1:50 - 1:56)
"So here are the five hacks. Write the brief once. Add taste on purpose. Feed assets, not adjectives. Build in passes. Review like a creative director. That is how you use AI to build websites that do not look AI-made."
